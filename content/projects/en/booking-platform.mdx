## The challenge

A multi-location service business ran its bookings through shared spreadsheets and a third-party calendar with no role-based access control: anyone with the link could view or edit any booking, with no record of who had changed what. Payments were reconciled by hand at month's end, which delayed catching errors.

## The approach

We designed the data model around three roles (front desk, location management, and admin), each with explicit permissions checked both in the interface and on the server — never only on the client. Every change to a booking (creation, edit, cancellation) is logged with who made it and when, queryable from the panel itself without direct database access.

Payments integrate with the gateway the business already used, rather than replacing it, to avoid forcing a migration of historical financial data. Availability checks (preventing double-bookings of the same resource) are enforced at the database level with exclusion constraints, not only in application logic, so they hold even under concurrent requests.

Before delivery, we reviewed the threat model for the payment flow and session handling against OWASP ASVS (level 2), paying particular attention to session fixation and cross-location customer data exposure.

## The outcome

The team stopped coordinating bookings over chat and parallel spreadsheets: each location sees only what applies to it, and payment reconciliation relies on a queryable log instead of being rebuilt by hand. The system remains in use and grows to new locations without changes to the permission model.
